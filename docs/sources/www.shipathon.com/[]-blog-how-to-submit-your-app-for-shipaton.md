You just published your app in stores, but there’s one job left: submitting it
to Shipaton. Or maybe you’re not yet there, since there’s still time left to
submit. No matter what the case, you should start looking at how to submit your
app to Shipaton at this point.

Shipaton submissions are sent through
[Devpost](https://www.shipaton.com/go/devpost?to=%2F) . The submission form has
five sections: Manage team, Project overview, Project details, Additional info,
and Submit. Here’s how to get through each step without a headache.

The submission deadline is Sep 30, 2026 at 11:45pm PDT. You can save a draft and
edit a submitted project until the deadline, but don’t leave the final click
until the final minute.

## First, make sure your app is eligible

**For the main Shipaton competition** (all other categories than Next Gen), your
app must:

- Must be an iOS, iPadOS, macOS, or Android app, web apps are not eligible
- Use the RevenueCat SDK to power at least one in-app or web purchase, or serve
  ads through RevenueCat Ads
- Be a new app whose first public version was released during the Shipaton
  submission window
- Be fully published on the Apple App Store, Google Play Store, Mac App Store,
  or Samsung Galaxy Store
- Work as shown in your submission video and description
- Be available to download in the United States

**Apps released before Shipaton don’t qualify**, and moving an existing app to
another store doesn’t make it new. An existing web app can qualify if your
Shipaton submission is iOS, iPadOS, macOS, or Android version of that app, that
user can download from the mentioned App Stores.

**TestFlight or testing-track build doesn’t count**. Shipaton needs a live store
listing, and app review can take days, so don’t leave it until deadline week.

Students entering the Next Gen Award get an exception: they can submit a demo
video and public open-source repository instead of a store listing. You can
enter both Next Gen Award and any of the other categories, by providing a public
open-source repository, demo video, and an app store link.

## Prepare these materials for your submission

Get these assets ready for your submission. All of them are required and
**missing any of them will make your app not eligible for judging**:

- Your project name and a short tagline
- A description of the app and its main features
- A public App Store, Google Play, Mac App Store, or Galaxy Store URL
- A public YouTube or Vimeo demo video
- A 1024 × 1024 app icon
- RevenueCat project ID
- At least one 1179 × 2556 app screenshot without a device frame
- A free trial or promo code that lets judges test all premium features
- Any category-specific links, IDs, metrics, or explanations

Some sponsor categories might have extra requirements, see official rules for
requirements on those. Everything you submit needs to be in English.

### How to find your RevenueCat project id

Devpost submission requires you to attach your RevenueCat project ID, you can
find your project ID in the
[RevenueCat dashboard](https://app.revenuecat.com/overview) , by navigating to
the **Project settings** from the sidebar after selecting your app. The project
ID is also visible in the URL.

## Completing the Devpost submission

Register for Shipaton, start a project, and save it as a draft. Devpost splits
the submission into five steps.

### Step 1: manage your team

Start by adding everyone who worked on the app.

Solo project? You’re done here. For a team project, invite people with the email
tied to their Devpost account or send them Devpost’s private invitation link.
Add everyone before the deadline.

### Step 2: complete the project overview

Project overview is the card people will see in the Devpost gallery. It asks
for:

- **Project name:** use the same recognizable name as your store listing
- **Tagline:** explain what the app does in one short sentence
- **Thumbnail:** upload a clean image that remains readable at a small size —
  Devpost recommends a 3:2 image in JPG, PNG, or GIF format

Here's my tip on how to do this well: skip taglines like “An AI-powered
productivity platform.” Say what the product actually does: “Practice difficult
conversations before having them with your team.”

### Step 3: tell your project’s story

Project details are the core of the submission: what you built, why it matters,
and a working demo.
[Devpost’s submission guide](https://help.devpost.com/article/126-know-your-submission-steps)
divides this into the project story, technology tags, links, images, and demo
video. Let’s go over each part in detail:

#### Write a description judges can scan

Answer the basics first:

1. What problem does it solve?
2. Who is it for?
3. What does it let them do?
4. How does it make money?
5. What makes it interesting or different?
6. Which award categories are you targeting?

Add the build story: what was hard, what you learned, and what comes next. Break
it up with headings and short paragraphs. You can use AI to correct spelling and
formatting, but **don’t let AI write your whole description. No one will like
reading that.**

Include the proof each category asks for. Make the job for judges easier by
explaining why your app qualifies for selected categories, and what makes it
stand out.

#### Add your technology tags

Tag RevenueCat and the main tools you used. Devpost allows up to 25 tags. If
you’re entering a sponsor category, tag that product too: Kotlin Multiplatform,
Replit, OneSignal, Layers, Stripe, or whichever one applies.

These are mainly used for explaining your project, they don’t affect judging.

#### Add the public app link

For a standard entry, link to the fully published app on:

- Apple’s App Store or Mac App Store
- Google Play
- Samsung Galaxy Store

Test the store link in a private browser window. The app needs to be
downloadable in the US.

The store-link exception applies only to Next Gen. In any other case **you need
to have a store link in your submission to be considered eligible**.

#### Upload your images

For Shipaton, that means:

- A 1024 × 1024 app icon
- At least one screenshot at 1179 × 2556
- Screenshots without device frames

Screenshot should show the core experience, how you’re using RevenueCat, and the
product’s strongest points. If you end up winning we will use the app icon and
one of the screenshots in the marketing materials.

#### Add your demo video

Upload the video to YouTube or Vimeo. Unlisted YouTube videos are fine; private
ones won’t be viewable by the judges, so don’t do that.

Keep the essential demo under two minutes as **judges aren’t required to watch
beyond the two minutes.** Show the app running on the device it was built for,
and highlight the most important features and aspects of your app.

In the first two minutes, cover:

- Your app’s elevator pitch
- The app’s core experience in use
- Its purchase, subscription, web-payment, or advertising experience
- The prize categories you are targeting and why the app fits them

Don’t spend the week making a trailer for your app or game. A clear screen
recording with concise narration beats a long intro or elaborate title sequence.

As explained in
[How we judge Shipaton](https://www.shipaton.com/blog/how-we-judge-shipaton) ,
judges make most of their decision from the description, video, and screenshots.
Lead with the stuff that makes your app stand out, and mention which categories
you are targeting.

If you haven’t made one before, keep it simple: record the screen, add a clear
voice-over, and tell one coherent story. Our
[guide to storytelling](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/how-to-win-shipaton-part-4-pitching-your-app)
can help you structure the pitch. Avoid copyrighted music, third-party
trademarks, and other protected material unless you have permission to use them.
**For the influencer categories, do not use the likeness or brand of the
influencers.**

### Step 4: choose your categories and complete the additional information

Additional info holds the Shipaton-specific fields: media, store links, your
RevenueCat project ID, and any promo code judges need for premium features.

You can enter several compatible categories, but don’t try to target each
category. Each one may need its own explanation, link, account ID, or proof.
Pick the ones your app is best suited for.

The [official Shipaton rules](https://www.shipaton.com/go/devpost?to=%2Frules)
contain the complete category requirements and judging criteria.

### RevenueCat awards

- **Grand Prize:** share what happened after launch, using the numbers that best
  make your case: downloads, revenue, active users, paid customers, conversion,
  retention, or something else.
- **HAMM Award**: explain the business model, pricing, paywall, conversion and
  revenue, including what you changed or learned.
- **Catvertising Award**: show where RevenueCat Ads appear, why those placements
  fit the experience, and how ads work alongside other revenue.
- **RevenueCat Design Award**: for exceptional craft, interaction design and
  animation. Point judges to the details worth noticing.
- **RevenueCat Peace Prize**: for apps with a positive impact on people,
  communities or society. Be specific about who benefits and what changes for
  them.
- **Best Game Award**: for the strongest mobile game. Cover the gameplay loop,
  progression or replayability, art direction and why the monetization belongs.
- **#BuildInPublic Award**: for the best public build story. Link to your posts
  and show how feedback, accountability or the community changed the app.
- **Next Gen Award**: for qualifying student projects. Use an academic email and
  submit a demo plus a public repository with code, setup instructions and a
  visible open-source license.
- **Conflict of Interest Award**: only for eligible RevenueCat and sponsor
  employees.

All eligible projects can be considered for the Grand Prize, but you still need
to show what happened after launch. Revenue made by the app is used for the
shortlist, but it does not decide the winner. Making the most money won't
automatically win you the Grand Prize.

### Influencer awards

The five briefs are:

- **Productivity — Christopher Lawley:** a focused tool for Apple power users to
  save and retrieve reusable text, documents, files, and images
- **Nutrition and Healthy Eating — Abbey’s Kitchen:** a flexible nutrition app
  inspired by the Hunger Crushing Combo framework, without calorie counting or
  restrictive meal plans
- **Yoga and Fitness — Simone Sharice:** a personalized companion that turns
  movement, Pilates, recovery, and wellness needs into a clear daily plan
- **Career Coaching — Leadership Heather:** a practice environment for new
  managers preparing for difficult workplace conversations
- **Gaming — Mr Lewis Blogs Gaming:** a gaming bucket-list app for saving,
  organizing, completing, rating, and sharing games

You can enter one Influencer Award per project and still enter other eligible
categories. State which brief you chose and how the app fits that audience in
the Devpost submission.

Building for an influencer’s audience doesn’t give you permission to use their
name, image, voice, logo or likeness. Get express written consent before using
any of it in the product, store listing or marketing.

### Sponsor awards

- **Ship Kotlin Everywhere — JetBrains:** publish for both iOS and Android using
  Kotlin Multiplatform, Compose Multiplatform, or both. Include both store links
  and explain the implementation.
- **Most Viral App — Noise:** use Noise to promote the live app during Shipaton.
  Include the app URL and the email associated with your Noise account.
- **Best App for Galaxy — Samsung:** publish on the Galaxy Store and explain
  Galaxy-specific work such as foldable support, Samsung features, or device
  optimization.
- **Idea to Income — Replit:** build and publish using Replit and RevenueCat
  through Replit Agent. Include at least three public build posts, the Replit
  preview URL, username, and an explanation of Replit’s role.
- **Keep Them Coming Back — OneSignal:** integrate OneSignal and deploy at least
  one campaign. Include the OneSignal App ID and describe the messaging
  experience.
- **The Growth Loop Award — Layers:** install the Layers SDK and describe the
  audience, hypothesis, experiment, observed signal, lessons, and next step.
- **Funnel Vision Award — Stripe:** launch a live web-to-app funnel using
  RevenueCat Funnels with Stripe. Include the funnel URL and Stripe Project ID.

Only enter a sponsor category once the integration works and you have every
requested detail. A technology tag on its own won’t qualify.

### Step 5: review and submit

The last screen is a short rules-and-terms confirmation.

Before you press Submit project, check:

- Is the store link live and available in the US?
- Does the app install and behave like the version in your video?
- Is the video public and is the essential footage under two minutes?
- Have you uploaded the correctly sized icon and screenshot?
- Can judges access premium functionality through a free trial or promo code?
- Have you answered the additional questions for every selected category?
- Are your teammate details correct?
- Are your written materials and testing instructions in English, or accompanied
  by an English translation?

After submitting, click View and inspect the public entry. Check the formatting,
embedded video, gallery, links and category answers.

You’re only done when Devpost shows Submitted and 5/5 steps done. A saved draft
doesn’t count.

## You can still edit after submitting

Submitting early doesn’t lock the entry. You can make changes until the
deadline. After that, edits to your Devpost portfolio won’t change the version
entered into Shipaton.

That extra time is useful to:

- Catch private or broken video links
- Fix missing fields
- Improve your description
- Replace weak screenshots
- Add post-launch growth results
- Confirm that the store listing and promo code work

You can submit more than one app, but each entry must be unique and
substantially different. When yours is ready, submit it and click View for one
last look at the public entry.

## Frequently asked questions

Still unsure whether your app qualifies? Here are the answers to some of the
most common submission questions. See also our
[frequently asked questions section on shipaton.com](https://www.shipaton.com/faq)
if your questions are not answered here.

### Can i submit more than one app?

Yes. Each app must be a separate, eligible project whose first store release
happens during the Shipaton window, from August 1 to September 30, 2026.

### Does releasing an existing app on a second store count as a new launch?

No. If the app was already available in one app store before August 1, releasing
it in another store during Shipaton does not make it a new app.

There is an exception for web apps. A project that previously existed only on
the web can qualify if its first native app-store release happens during the
Shipaton window.

### What if my app is still waiting for store review at the deadline?

It will not qualify. Judges need to be able to download the published app from a
supported store.

Store review can take several business days—and longer if the build is
rejected—so RevenueCat recommends submitting it for review at least one week
before the Shipaton deadline.

### Can i participate without an Apple or Google developer account?

Possibly, through the Next Gen Award. Eligible students can submit a demo and
public open-source repository instead of publishing the app to a store.

You will need a verifiable academic email address from a school, university,
bootcamp, or another academic program. Standard Shipaton categories still
require a published app and working RevenueCat integration.

### Can i enter the next gen award and another category?

Yes, provided you meet both sets of requirements. That means submitting the code
required for Next Gen and publishing the app to a supported store for the other
category.

The store release is not considered when judging the Next Gen Award itself.

### Does every submission need to use RevenueCat?

Yes. Standard entries must use the RevenueCat SDK for purchases, subscriptions,
or RevenueCat Ads.

### Is there a limit to the number of teammates?

No. Your team can have any number of members. However, if an eligible prize
includes travel to New York, RevenueCat will fly one team member.
