## Overview of Shipaton 2026

- Tagline: "The mobile hackathon that's all about shipping a ton. Ship a
  brand-new app to the App Store, Google Play Store, or Samsung Galaxy Store
  between August 1st and September 30th, 2026, and compete for over $700,000
  in cash prizes." ([devpost], intro)
- Positioning: "The World's Biggest Mobile Hackathon for People Who Actually
  Ship"; "Shipaton 2026 is RevenueCat's global hackathon for builders
  launching real apps to real stores, competing for big prizes, and showing
  the world what they shipped." ([home], title and intro)
- Organizer: RevenueCat, announcing "the return of our global hackathon"
  after "last year's Shipaton" ([devpost], Overview). Registration and
  submission run on Devpost ([home], Join Shipaton 2026 on Devpost). Questions
  go to the hackathon manager at `shipaton@revenuecat.com` ([devpost], See
  also).
- Format: online, with optional in-person events. "Shipaton is online, but it
  does not have to feel remote." ([home], Shipaton IRL brings builders
  together around the world)
- Eligible stores: "Apple's App Store, Google Play Store, or Samsung Galaxy
  Store"; platforms: "iOS, macOS, or Android apps" ([faq], Submissions) or
  "iOS, iPadOS, macOS, or Android" ([devpost], Requirements).
- Headline prizes on the site: "**$740k+ in cash prizes**", "Invitation to
  App Growth Annual in NYC", "Your app featured on a giant Times Square
  billboard", "Shippy trophy", "Blog feature plus 9to5Mac and 9to5Google
  spotlight" ([home], Win cash, a times square billboard, and more). The site
  advertises "21 categories" ([home], 21 categories).
- Prize pool: "over $700,000 in cash prizes and truly unique rewards — over
  $1 million worth of prizes in total" ([devpost], Overview); "Over $700,000
  in cash prizes — and growing as more sponsors join" ([devpost], Bigger and
  better).
- The challenge, verbatim ([devpost], The challenge):
  - "Ship a brand-new app to the App Store, Google Play Store, or — new this
    year — the Samsung Galaxy Store, between August 1st and September 30th,
    2026."
  - "Integrate the RevenueCat SDK to power at least one in-app purchase or
    serve ads through RevenueCat Ads."
- "Updates to previously released apps won't qualify ... You can brainstorm,
  build, and post before August 1st, but your app must be released for the
  first time during the Shipaton window." ([devpost], The challenge)
- New for 2026 per [devpost] (The challenge; Bigger and better; Categories):
  Samsung Galaxy Store as an eligible store; "\[NEW\]" Next Gen, Catvertising,
  and Best Game awards; "NEW: Investor exposure through the Shipaton Growth
  Fund"; flights to New York City for the Shippies ceremony and App Growth
  Annual; winners featured on Times Square billboards; press coverage on
  9to5Mac and 9to5Google.
- Also new in 2026 per [blog-2026] (Jul 2, 2026, by Perttu Lähteenlahti and
  Charlie Chapman): "a brand-new student category" (Next Gen) and "New this
  year: the #shipatonsale". Its summary: "ship a new iOS or Android app this
  August–September and compete for over $1 million in prizes."
- Growing the product after 1.0 is expected: "Your app can be something you
  just started working on, or something you've been working on for a longer
  time but just haven't shipped yet." "We recommend shipping your 1.0 as
  quickly as possible to get through App Review, then pushing updates from
  there." ([blog-2026], The challenge)
- Scale of the series: "Across the last two years of Shipaton and Shipyard,
  around 2,000 apps have made it to the app stores and have collectively made
  $7,808,524 in revenue. And we've handed out a total of $545,500 in prize
  money." ([blog-judge], intro)
- 2025 baseline: "Close to a thousand new apps launched" ([blog-2026], Bigger
  than ever); Devpost says "Hundreds of new apps launched" ([devpost], Bigger
  and better). The 2025 winners post counts "812 projects" submitted
  ([winners-2025]).
- Full 2025-to-2026 comparison:
  [What changed from 2025 to 2026](#what-changed-from-2025-to-2026).

## Eligibility rules

### Who may enter

- Countries: "Depending on where you reside, United States law or your local
  laws may prohibit you from participating in a competition or receiving a
  prize." "New this year, **individuals residing in Quebec or Brazil ARE
  allowed to participate** in the Shipaton." Residents of "Cuba, Iran, North
  Korea, Crimea, and Russia" "may be excluded from participation or prize
  eligibility". ([faq], Eligibility and participation)
- Team size: "Is there any limit to the number of teammates?" "No. Though
  we'll only fly one of you to NY when you win an eligible prize." ([faq],
  Eligibility and participation)
- Minors: "Active students who are at least 13 can enter the Next Gen
  Award." A parent or legal guardian "must review and agree to the official
  rules on your behalf" and complete the guardian consent form; "Signed
  written consent will be required before you can be announced as a winner."
  ([faq], Eligibility and participation)
- "Minor entrants, and teams that include a minor entrant, may compete only
  for the Next Gen Award." ([faq], Eligibility and participation)
- Students at or over the age of majority "may qualify for the Next Gen Award
  and another category if they meet both categories' requirements." ([faq],
  Eligibility and participation)
- Next Gen proof of student status: "a verifiable academic email address from
  a high school, college, university, bootcamp, or another academic program"
  ([faq], Eligibility and participation); Devpost says "active students with
  a .edu (or equivalent) email address" ([devpost], Judging criteria).
- Non-students need store accounts: "To participate in Shipaton outside of
  the Next Gen Award, you must be able to integrate the RevenueCat SDK into
  your project, which includes setting up products in the relevant app
  stores." ([faq], Eligibility and participation)
- RevenueCat and sponsor employees compete in the Conflict of Interest Award
  ([devpost], Categories), "a special non-cash category" that lets them take
  part "without competing for the standard cash prizes" ([cat-coi]).
- Age for all other categories: no explicit minimum is captured. Because
  minors "may compete only for the Next Gen Award" ([faq]), entrants in
  other categories must have reached the age of majority where they live;
  the exact clause is in the uncaptured official rules.
- Organizations: no source says whether companies may enter; the only
  related text is that store developer accounts can be individual or
  organization accounts ([late-review]).

### Which apps qualify

- New apps only: "Updates to previously released apps won't qualify"; "your
  app must be released for the first time during the Shipaton window"
  ([devpost], The challenge).
- Pre-work is allowed: "You can start building or even promoting your app
  before then, but you should not publish it to the app stores before August
  1 if you want it to remain eligible." ([faq], Submissions)
- Multiple apps: "Can I submit multiple apps?" "Yes." ([faq], Submissions)
- Second-store launches do not count: "Launching it on a second store does
  not count if it was already live in another app store." Example: live on
  Google Play before August 1, then App Store after August 1, "would not
  qualify." ([faq], Submissions)
- Web-app exception: "If your project only existed on the web before
  Shipaton, you may submit a new app-store version as long as its first store
  release happens during the event window." ([faq], Submissions)
- Must be live, not in review: "My app is still under review on the store.
  Can I still win?" "No. Your app must be published on the app stores so
  judges can download and review it." ([faq], Submissions)
- Main-competition checklist (all categories except Next Gen), per
  [blog-submit] (First, make sure your app is eligible). The app must:
  - "Must be an iOS, iPadOS, macOS, or Android app, web apps are not
    eligible"
  - "Use the RevenueCat SDK to power at least one in-app or web purchase,
    or serve ads through RevenueCat Ads"
  - "Be a new app whose first public version was released during the
    Shipaton submission window"
  - "Be fully published on the Apple App Store, Google Play Store, Mac App
    Store, or Samsung Galaxy Store"
  - "Work as shown in your submission video and description"
  - "Be available to download in the United States"
- "**TestFlight or testing-track build doesn't count**. Shipaton needs a live
  store listing" ([blog-submit]).
- Multiple entries: "You can submit more than one app, but each entry must be
  unique and substantially different." ([blog-submit], You can still edit
  after submitting)
- Next Gen plus other categories: "You can enter both Next Gen Award and any
  of the other categories, by providing a public open-source repository, demo
  video, and an app store link." ([blog-submit]) Minors are still limited to
  Next Gen ([faq]).
- Language: "Everything you submit needs to be in English." ([blog-submit],
  Prepare these materials)
- The US-download and English rules appear only in 2026 blog posts
  ([blog-submit]; [late-review] says "in the US app stores"), not on the
  Devpost capture or the FAQ.

## Submission requirements

### Build requirements

- "Entrants must create a working software application that uses the
  RevenueCat SDK to power at least one in-app or web purchase (each a
  'Project'). Apps must be built for iOS, iPadOS, macOS, or Android."
  ([devpost], Requirements > What to build)
- Next Gen students "follow a different, lower-friction path: submit a video
  and source code instead of a published store listing. No paid Apple or
  Google developer account required." ([devpost], Requirements > What to
  build)
- SDK: "All Shipaton submissions must use the RevenueCat SDK for in-app
  purchases, subscriptions, or ads." ([faq], Submissions)
- Stores: "your app must be submitted to one of the supported app stores:
  Apple's App Store, Google Play Store, or Samsung Galaxy Store." ([faq],
  Submissions)

### Devpost submission checklist

Every submission must include ([devpost], Requirements > What to submit on
Devpost):

- A "**text description** of your app's features and functionality."
- A "**demo video** of your project", which:
  - "Should show the app running on the device it was built for."
  - "Must be no longer than 2 minutes of essential footage — **judges are
    not required to watch beyond two minutes.**"
  - "Must be uploaded to and publicly visible on YouTube or Vimeo, with the
    link provided on this form."
  - "Must not include third-party trademarks or copyrighted music/material
    unless you have permission to use it."
- A "**URL to your fully published app** on the App Store or Google Play or
  Samsung Galaxy Store."
- A "**1024×1024 app icon**."
- "At least **one screenshot** of the app at **1179px × 2556px** with **no
  device frame**."
- "Either a **free trial** in your app, **or** a **promo code** so judges can
  unlock the in-app purchase and test all premium features."

The 2026 submission guide ([blog-submit], Prepare these materials) says all
of these are required and "**missing any of them will make your app not
eligible for judging**". It adds three items that the Devpost capture does
not list: a project name and short tagline, the **RevenueCat project ID**, and
"Any category-specific links, IDs, metrics, or explanations". Full list:

- "Your project name and a short tagline"
- "A description of the app and its main features"
- "A public App Store, Google Play, Mac App Store, or Galaxy Store URL"
- "A public YouTube or Vimeo demo video"
- "A 1024 × 1024 app icon"
- "RevenueCat project ID" (find it in the RevenueCat dashboard under
  "**Project settings**"; it "is also visible in the URL")
- "At least one 1179 × 2556 app screenshot without a device frame"
- "A free trial or promo code that lets judges test all premium features"
- "Any category-specific links, IDs, metrics, or explanations"

Judging intake also checks for "a valid bundle ID or package name, which we
can use to check that the app has the RevenueCat SDK integrated correctly"
and "The required marketing materials for Times Square ... (app icon and
screenshots)" ([blog-judge], Stage 1).

### Devpost form walkthrough

The form "has five sections: Manage team, Project overview, Project details,
Additional info, and Submit" ([blog-submit], intro). Step by step, per
[blog-submit]:

1.  **Manage team.** Add everyone who worked on the app; invite by the email
    tied to their Devpost account or Devpost's private invitation link. "Add
    everyone before the deadline."
1.  **Project overview** (the gallery card). "Project name: use the same
    recognizable name as your store listing"; "Tagline: explain what the app
    does in one short sentence"; "Thumbnail: ... Devpost recommends a 3:2
    image in JPG, PNG, or GIF format".
1.  **Project details.** Story, tags, links, images, and video:
    - Description answers: "What problem does it solve?", "Who is it for?",
      "What does it let them do?", "How does it make money?", "What makes it
      interesting or different?", "Which award categories are you
      targeting?" Add the build story. "**don't let AI write your whole
      description.**" Explain why the app qualifies for each selected
      category.
    - Technology tags: "Devpost allows up to 25 tags"; tag RevenueCat and any
      sponsor product. Tags "don't affect judging".
    - Public app link: "Test the store link in a private browser window. The
      app needs to be downloadable in the US." Outside Next Gen, "**you need
      to have a store link in your submission to be considered eligible**".
    - Images: "A 1024 × 1024 app icon", "At least one screenshot at 1179 ×
      2556", "Screenshots without device frames". "If you end up winning we
      will use the app icon and one of the screenshots in the marketing
      materials."
    - Video: "Unlisted YouTube videos are fine; private ones won't be
      viewable by the judges". In the first two minutes cover the elevator
      pitch, the core experience, "Its purchase, subscription, web-payment,
      or advertising experience", and "The prize categories you are
      targeting and why the app fits them". "A clear screen recording with
      concise narration beats a long intro or elaborate title sequence."
      "**For the influencer categories, do not use the likeness or brand of
      the influencers.**"
1.  **Additional info and categories.** "Additional info holds the
    Shipaton-specific fields: media, store links, your RevenueCat project
    ID, and any promo code judges need for premium features." "You can enter
    several compatible categories, but don't try to target each category."
1.  **Review and submit.** "The last screen is a short rules-and-terms
    confirmation." Pre-submit checks: store link live and available in the
    US; app behaves like the video; video public and essential footage under
    two minutes; correct icon and screenshot sizes; judges can reach premium
    features; every selected category's questions answered; teammate details
    correct; materials "in English, or accompanied by an English
    translation". Then click View and inspect the public entry.

- "You're only done when Devpost shows Submitted and 5/5 steps done. A saved
  draft doesn't count." ([blog-submit], Step 5)
- Editing: "Submitting early doesn't lock the entry. You can make changes
  until the deadline. After that, edits to your Devpost portfolio won't
  change the version entered into Shipaton." ([blog-submit], You can still
  edit after submitting)
- Category answers gate judging: "If you left the question for the
  RevenueCat Peace Prize empty, your app won't get judged for that."
  ([blog-judge], Stage 1)

### App review timing and late approvals

- "App review usually takes a few business days, assuming your app is not
  rejected. We recommend submitting your app to the app stores at least one
  week before the Shipaton deadline" ([faq], Submissions).
- "Store review can take several business days—and longer if the build is
  rejected—so RevenueCat recommends submitting it for review at least one
  week before the Shipaton deadline." ([blog-submit], FAQ)
- Still in review at the deadline: "It will not qualify. Judges need to be
  able to download the published app from a supported store." ([blog-submit],
  FAQ); "No. Your app must be published on the app stores" ([faq]).

From the 2026 late-submitter guide ([late-review], Sep 3, 2026, by Perttu
Lähteenlahti):

- Buffer: the app must be public "in the US app stores by September 30th at
  11:45 pm PDT"; "Apple also says that it can take up to 24 hours for the app
  to appear in the App Store, so ... your app should be in stores, with
  RevenueCat powering an in-app purchase or serving ads through RevenueCat
  Ads, at least a day or two before the submission period ends."
- Accounts: "Apple charges $99 per year, while Google Play charges a
  one-time $25 registration fee." "Organization accounts require additional
  verification, which may include a D-U-N-S number."
- Google Play's testing rule: it "applies to personal Play Console accounts
  created after Nov 13, 2023. Before you can apply for production access,
  you must run a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in continuously
  for 14 days." Discord has a `#looking-for-google-play-tester` channel.
  Derived: a new personal Play account that starts a closed test on
  2026-09-21 cannot finish 14 days before the deadline.
- Monetization in the first build: shipping free first and adding purchases
  later "only makes sense if you have enough time for two reviews"; "If the
  deadline is close, including monetization in the first submission may be
  safer than betting on a second review." Test purchases in the sandboxes
  first.
- Review hygiene: real privacy policy (it "needs to match what you submitted
  in the privacy declaration"); Terms of Use links for subscriptions (Apple's
  standard EULA link is given); no "beta" or "number 1" claims; a test
  account without 2FA and without "an active entitlement" so reviewers see
  the paywall; "If your app uses Google or other third-party authentication,
  make sure you've also included Sign in with Apple, as that is a
  requirement"; a screen recording "under a minute in length" of purchases
  and login.
- Resubmitting: Apple "does not promise to review submissions in the order
  received"; on Google, "Sending additional changes while an app is under
  review may push it to the back of the review queue." "Don't restart review
  for a minor improvement."
- Expedited review: "RevenueCat's official guidance is not to use expedited
  review for this."
- Plan: "focus on getting your app in review in the next two weeks, and
  preferably in the next week. Stop developing new features." (written about
  four weeks before the deadline)
- Students can skip the stores via Next Gen "with a demo video and public
  open-source repository".

## Judging process and criteria

### Judging criteria sources

- Per-category criteria are in [devpost] (Judging criteria) and on each
  category page; they are summarized under
  [Prize categories and prize structure](#prize-categories-and-prize-structure).
  [devpost] defers to the uncaptured official rules: "See official rules for
  full judging criteria."
- Grand Prize consideration is automatic but proof is not: "All eligible
  projects can be considered for the Grand Prize, but you still need to show
  what happened after launch. Revenue made by the app is used for the
  shortlist, but it does not decide the winner. Making the most money won't
  automatically win you the Grand Prize." ([blog-submit], RevenueCat awards)
- Best App for Galaxy "Entries follow the standard Shipaton judging
  criteria" ([cat-galaxy]); the corpus never defines those standard criteria.

### Judging funnel and stages

From [blog-judge] (2026 post, "subject to change"):

- No early judging: because entries can be edited "until the very last
  minute" and many arrive late, "we haven't done that, and we won't."
- **Intake filtering (October 1st).** Submissions are exported from Devpost
  to an internal tool that verifies: a store link ("This year we're
  validating other stores and web purchase integrations as well"); "a valid
  bundle ID or package name" used to check the RevenueCat SDK; all required
  fields answered; a video; "The required marketing materials for Times
  Square ... (app icon and screenshots)". "Sadly, but unsurprisingly, a lot
  of project submissions get filtered out at this point." Projects are also
  tagged for the categories whose questions they answered.
- **Prescreening.** Each entry goes to "a minimum of two RevenueCat
  screeners" who score it "from 1-5 in all the categories the app is
  targeting" after they "Watch the first 2 minutes of the submission video"
  and "Read the project submission and the answers". Screeners are "not
  required to download the app".
- **Judge scoring.** "We don't tell which judge is judging which category of
  apps, so that no one tries to reach the judges directly". Each judge must
  "Read the entire description", "Watch at least two minutes of the required
  video", "Review all of the screenshots", and "Score the app from 1 to 5 (5
  being best) in each category"; downloading is welcome "(it's encouraged), but
  not required". Each judge then "nominates their top apps per category"; "close
  to 100 apps still make it to this final round of judging."
- **Final winner selection (October 8-9).** "RevenueCat (or the category's
  sponsor) meets to deliberate and pick a first, second, and third place
  from the judges' nominees"; they re-read the description, re-watch the
  video, review screenshots, and "at least one RevenueCat developer advocate
  downloads the app to confirm it matches what the video shows."
- Afterwards RevenueCat contacts "first-place winners to start coordinating
  their flights to App Growth Annual in NYC" and uses the submitted icons and
  screenshots for marketing.
- What screeners want in the first two minutes of video: "What your app is
  about, e.g. the elevator pitch", "Your app in use", "How and why your app
  is targeting the different prize categories". "don't try to jam your app
  into every prize category." Longer videos are allowed "since the videos
  become public when winners are published", but "pay careful attention to
  the first two minutes."
- Volume: "Last year, that meant reviewing a thousand projects in a week".
- Deadline clustering chart (series "Shipaton 2024", "Shipaton 2025",
  "Shipyard"; the capture does not say which series the numbers belong to):
  "8 weeks before deadline" 15%, 7 weeks 7%, 6 weeks 7%, 5 weeks 5%, 4 weeks
  7%, 3 weeks 12%, 2 weeks 7%, 1 week 11%, "Final week" 30%. "In 2025 25% of
  Shipyard projects were submitted in the last 3 days!"

### RevenueCat core category requirements

- **Grand Prize.** "honors the app that shows the strongest user traction and
  growth momentum during Shipaton. Judges want to see what you did after
  launch to move the app forward: product improvements, user acquisition,
  retention, revenue, and a clear story of momentum." "This is the broadest
  category." Include: what you did after launch to grow; "Relevant numbers,
  such as downloads, revenue, conversion, active users, waitlist growth,
  retention, social reach, or community engagement"; screenshots or copy
  showing what changed; what you learned and would do next. ([cat-grand])
- **#BuildInPublic Award.** For an "interesting development journey on social
  media or in online communities"; "Audience size does not matter. The
  quality and usefulness of the journey does." Include: links to accounts or
  specific posts; how building in public helped; examples of feedback;
  "Posts tagged `#Shipaton` and optionally `#BuildInPublic` where
  appropriate." Judges "consider whether public feedback led to changes that
  made the app better." ([cat-bip])
- **HAMM Award.** "the smartest use of RevenueCat to drive real revenue.
  Judges are looking for a well-crafted paywall, thoughtful pricing and
  packaging, strong conversion, and monetization that genuinely fits the
  product." Include: monetization strategy; "Your paywall, pricing,
  packaging, and trial approach"; conversion, revenue, retention, or purchase
  data; how RevenueCat helped. ([cat-hamm])
- **Catvertising Award (new).** "the most creative and effective use of
  RevenueCat Ads as a monetization method"; "The strongest submissions treat
  ads as part of the product strategy, not a bolt-on." Include: how the app
  uses RevenueCat Ads; placements and why; how ads work alongside
  subscriptions, purchases, or trials; early signal the ads don't harm UX.
  ([cat-cat])
- **RevenueCat Design Award.** "celebrates the craft of app development,
  separate from business viability"; "This category is about taste, care,
  and execution." Include: unique design elements; screens, flows,
  interactions, or animations to notice; how design supports the product;
  "A demo video that clearly shows the app in motion." ([cat-design])
- **RevenueCat Peace Prize.** "the project with the strongest social good";
  explain "who the app helps, why the problem matters, and how the product
  design supports that impact." Include: audience served; problem and why it
  matters; impact design; "early evidence of usefulness, adoption, feedback,
  or real-world benefit." ([cat-peace])
- **Best Game Award (new).** "a mobile game that feels fun, polished, and
  thoughtfully monetized"; "a monetization model that fits the genre instead
  of fighting it"; "Your game does not need to be huge." Include: core
  gameplay loop; art direction and tone; monetization fit; "A demo video that
  shows real gameplay on the device the game was built for." ([cat-game])
- **Next Gen Award (new).** "for active students aged 13 and older with a
  `.edu` or equivalent academic email address"; "submitted with a demo video
  and public open-source code"; "does not require a paid Apple or Google
  developer account, and no App Store or Google Play release is required."
  Include: demo video; "A link to a public open-source code repository"; "An
  open source license file in the repository"; description; for minors, the
  guardian's "name and email address and confirmation of their consent."
  ([cat-next-gen])
- **Conflict of Interest Award.** "gives builders connected to the event a
  place to participate without competing for the standard cash prizes."
  Include: description, demo video, context on your connection to
  RevenueCat or sponsors, and "Source code, app links, screenshots, and
  testing access where applicable." ([cat-coi])

### Influencer award briefs

Each influencer award is a fixed product brief with its own judging list.

- **Productivity: Christopher Lawley.** Build an app that "brings text
  snippets, images, files, and documents together in one fast, focused
  place" (canned email replies, documents shown to clients, form-filling
  info); "more purpose-built than a notes app without becoming a full file
  manager." Judges want one coherent experience, quick saving and finding,
  focus, "intelligent features and thoughtful interaction design", and the
  "care, speed, and utility that Apple power users expect." Audience: Apple
  power users; YouTube "230K"; "91.6% male"; top country "United States
  (29.6%)". ([cat-lawley])
- **Nutrition & Healthy Eating: Abbey's Kitchen (Abbey Sharp).** A "flexible
  app inspired by Abbey's **Hunger Crushing Combo** framework" answering
  "What can we add to make this meal more satisfying?"; "It must not rely on
  calorie counting or macro tracking." Judges want flexibility, messy
  real-life situations, an additive approach, no diet-culture patterns, and
  "compassionate, practical" design. Audience: "85% female"; largest age
  group "35-44 (35%)"; YouTube "718K", TikTok "1.1M", Instagram "412K".
  ([cat-abbey])
- **Yoga & Fitness: Simone Sharice.** "a personalized daily wellness
  companion" answering what to do today, possibly combining "Pilates
  classes, recovery, and other parts of a person's journey into one
  practical plan". Judges want a clear plan for today, personalization
  "without creating more decisions or information overload", and
  consistency over advice. Audience: "94.8% female"; "United States
  (76.6%)"; YouTube "101K", TikTok "258K", Instagram "306K".
  ([cat-simone])
- **Career Coaching: Leadership Heather.** A place for new managers to
  "practise feedback, boundaries, saying no, and other high-stakes
  conversations"; "not a script generator" but "a **flight simulator for
  hard conversations**". Judges want active practice, realistic scenarios,
  useful feedback, authentic leadership, and a safe space. Audience:
  "75% female"; Instagram "375K+", TikTok "88K", YouTube "2.6K".
  ([cat-heather])
- **Gaming: Mr Lewis Blogs Gaming.** "Build a gaming bucket list where people
  can add games, mark them as complete, rate them, and share their lists with
  friends." Judges want effortless saving "in the moment of discovery", an
  enjoyable backlog, completing and rating, natural sharing, and gamer
  energy. Audience: "approximately 88% male"; YouTube "376K", TikTok "377K",
  Instagram "84.8K". ([cat-lewis])

  audience doesn't give you permission to use their name, image, voice, logo
  or likeness. Get express written consent before using any of it in the

- Publicity: "If you end up winning we will use the app icon and one of the

- Store compliance: Galaxy entries must "comply with the store's content
  guidelines" ([cat-galaxy], Requirements).

  directly to your bank account. Attending the NYC RAGA event in person is
  not required." Visa help is offered; "you may send someone on your behalf.
  You can also receive the award and prize fully remotely." ([faq], Prizes

  RevenueCat will fly one team member." ([blog-submit], FAQ)
- Minors: guardian consent via a form; "Signed written consent will be
  required before you can be announced as a winner." ([faq])
- Legal residence limits: "United States law or your local laws may prohibit
  you from participating in a competition or receiving a prize" ([faq]).
- AI use: AI-built apps are explicitly welcome. "Use the tool of your choice
  to prompt your way to a fully functioning app"; "Any AI tool. Any app idea.
  Just ship it." ([vibe-code]) The one limit is on the write-up: "You can use
  AI to correct spelling and formatting, but **don't let AI write your whole
  description.**" ([blog-submit]) Nothing in the corpus restricts AI-built
  apps or third-party tools; Replit's category requires using "Replit
  Agent" ([cat-replit]).
- Ineligibility and removal (2026): entries missing required materials are
  "not eligible for judging" ([blog-submit]); "a lot of project submissions
  get filtered out" at intake ([blog-judge]); apps still in review "will not
  qualify" ([blog-submit]); TestFlight or testing-track builds do not count
  ([blog-submit]); before winners are final, "at least one RevenueCat
  developer advocate downloads the app to confirm it matches what the video
  shows" ([blog-judge]).
- Open-source licensing: Next Gen repos need "An open source license file in
  the repository" ([cat-next-gen]) and "a visible open-source license"
  ([blog-submit]).
- IP ownership, taxes, publicity releases, and dispute terms: not stated in

### Patterns among past winners

My synthesis from the winner posts; each point cites examples:

- Personal, specific problems with an origin story: Gurwi, Heartbeat Hero,
  Hearing Buddy, MoodHaven, Tomo Japan ([winners-2025]); Shipyard's stated
  lesson is "focus" on "a specific audience, a clear problem"
  ([shipyard-winners]).
- Public building on several channels, with feedback visibly changing the
  app: Meshing, Food Sense, BJJ Evolve ([winners-2024]); Gurwi, Echo
  Reminder, Tomo Japan, Friendy+ ([winners-2025]).
- Concrete numbers in the write-up: users, reviews, MRR, installs, revenue,
  sessions ([winners-2025]).
- Monetization designed into the product: Karo's paywall and free-tier

## Winning playbook

Tips are attributed to their source and edition. The how-to-win series was
written for Shipaton 2025; the judging and submission posts are 2026.

### Ideation (how to win, part 1)

From [win-1] (Aug 4, 2025, Perttu Lähteenlahti, who "competed in over 70
hackathons and ended up winning 45 of them"):

- The three keys: "Solve a real problem", "For real people", "In a way that
  delivers delight or an 'aha' moment".
- Build what you need to build: "I need to build this, because I'm the only
  one who can get it right."
- Start from the problem, not the solution; talk to people "Not to pitch
  your idea—just to listen"; solve a single problem first ("Don't try to
  build a full menu—just focus on getting one dish right").
- Keep validating "until the very last day"; release while "still slightly
  embarrassed"; early on "Don't implement analytics"; ask what users would
  pay, since "Monetizing is also one of the requirements of Shipaton."
- Pick "one primary metric" (activation, retention, referral, or
  monetization).
- Aim for a "Minimum Lovable Product (MLP)": "ruthlessly cutting scope,
  while preserving the delight"; "You're building a tiny hut, but one with
  charm."

### Building fast (how to win, part 2)

From [win-2] (Aug 13, 2025, Perttu Lähteenlahti):

- The "4-8-24 approach": "4 hours to build the first version"; "8 hours to
  polish it enough to show real users"; "24 hours (spread over three days) to
  refine, package, and release."
- The 8-hour build is "a convincing fake"; in the 24-hour stage also create
  the store pages, icons, and screenshots, and announce the app is coming.
- "It's okay to be embarrassed about the version you're going to launch. If
  you're not, you're probably launching too late."
- After launch, "Aim for weekly updates", mostly bug fixes and polish.
- "Use AI to make things fast, then improve on it"; "The trick is not to ship
  AI output untouched". "speed without polish is just a race to mediocrity."

### Growth (how to win, part 3)

From [win-3] (Aug 22, 2025, Perttu Lähteenlahti):

- Frame growth as momentum: "The milestone to aim for is 100 paying
  customers during Shipaton. It's ambitious but realistic".
- Acquire: start with your validation network and a LinkedIn or Twitter
  post; then niche Reddit, Discord, and forum communities and
  micro-influencers ("A micro-influencer with a few hundred engaged followers
  can often drive more adoption than a big account"); cross-promote with
  other participants in the Shipaton Discord; ask people at IRL events to
  try the app; treat first users as VIPs (early-bird discount, "founding
  user" icon, Easter egg).
- Discoverability: ASO keywords in title and description each release;
  benefit-led copy, screenshots, and a short demo video; ask early users for
  reviews; ship specific release notes, not "Bug fixes and performance
  improvements".
- Retention: fix reported issues fast and tell the reporter; iterate
  visibly; thank early adopters personally.
- Feedback loop: lightweight in-app questions; a Discord or Slack group.
- "Winning Shipaton isn't just about hitting the biggest numbers. It's about
  showing clear signs of growth and proving that you've hustled".

### Pitching (how to win, part 4)

From [win-4] (Sep 5, 2025, Perttu Lähteenlahti). Its "max three minutes"
video rule is the 2025 rule; 2026 uses two minutes ([devpost]).

- "A Devpost submission isn't just a checklist of features. It's a pitch."
  Avoid "reducing your pitch to a feature list".
- Use Dan Harmon's Story Circle: Setup, Need, Go, Search, Find, Take,
  Return, Change.
- Video: "proof first, polish second"; open with Setup + Need in "the
  opening 15–20 seconds"; show the app running on the device; show numbers
  as "a quick before-and-after"; end with a one-line transformation and call
  to action; "deliver your hook and proof in the first 60–90 seconds."
- Description: open with a logline, "For \[who\], \[App Name\] helps \[job to
  be done\] by \[distinct approach\], so they can \[valuable outcome\]."; then
  one paragraph on the problem, one on how the app works, and one with
  evidence ("traction, test results, or signals from early users").

### Shipyard lessons (2026, different hackathon)

From [win-shipyard] (Jan 27, 2026) and [still-shipyard] (Feb 6, 2026), both
by Perttu Lähteenlahti, written for Shipyard's influencer briefs:

- Pick the brief first: "don't use come to this hackathon with an existing
  idea that you'll then try to make fit one of the briefs. That rarely
  works". Then talk to the brief's target audience. ([win-shipyard])
- Build with the same 4/8/24-hour cadence; "keep launching and relaunching
  your app ... never take more than day or two from getting those
  improvements in the hands of your customers." ([win-shipyard])
- "The most valuable learning you can get is talking to the users who
  stopped using your app". ([win-shipyard])
- The video and the written proposal "are the two things the judges will be
  scrutinizing the hardest"; don't write them "on the last night before
  submissions close". ([win-shipyard])
- Late starters can still compete with "a focused MVP"; lean on LLM APIs to
  turn messy input into structured data, on Firebase or Supabase instead of
  custom backends, and on "RevenueCat's virtual currencies" for credit-based
  AI features; differentiate with "a clear hook". ([still-shipyard])

### Build-in-public case studies and interviews

- **Gurwi (2025 #BuildInPublic winner)**, per [gurwi] (Sep 3, 2026):
  Camilo Peñalver started a separate English X account "where RevenueCat's
  team and the judges were most active", posted edited progress videos
  tagged #Shipaton and #BuildInPublic, and asked Spanish-speaking creators
  to share the story, for "more than four million views". "The judges chose
  Gurwi for the authenticity of its build-in-public story and the care
  visible in both the app and the videos around it." The team "submitted
  roughly three days before the deadline" but waited to post the form "to
  attach the most up to date app metrics and marketing results". His advice:
  "**tell a story**", "**build something original**", "**make the work
  visible**", and keep shipping. After the win, user payments passed
  "$19,000" in the year of the win.
- **Rudrank Riyam (2024 #buildinpublic winner, Meshing)**, per [rudrank]
  (Jul 31, 2025): he targeted one award from the start ("I only participated
  to win"), tweeted and posted YouTube videos daily, used Claude with SwiftUI
  docs to write code, and put the paywall on the payoff moment: the app "was
  free to use, but exporting a gradient as a wallpaper triggered a paywall".
  Advice: "Ship a ton."; don't obsess over perfection; solve problems that
  matter to you.
- **Mansour Mahamat (2024 #buildinpublic 3rd, BJJ Evolve)**, per [mansour]
  (Aug 22, 2025): built from a sport he knows (black belt); worked "at 5 a.m.
  every day before work"; a TikTok-fed waitlist "grew to 300 people in just
  two weeks"; "Building something is easy. Finding users is hard."; "Many
  others started building in public but stopped after 10 days."; "You can
  build the best app, but if no one sees it, you'll struggle."
- **Leandro Tolaini (2024 participant, not a winner)**, per [tolaini] (Jul
  28, 2025): first app, solo, built with SwiftUI and SwiftData; to hit the
  deadline he shipped "a 7-day free trial followed by a hard paywall";
  "Apple rejected the app several times"; earned "around $100-200". Advice:
  "just jump into it"; "Don't try to act like a company"; add "one small
  thing, like a fun animation or an unexpected feature".

### Conflicts that affect a submission

- **Deadline wording.** [faq]: "11:45 PM Pacific Time"; [blog-submit]:
  "11:45pm PDT"; [home]: "by September 30"; [devpost] capture: no time. These
  agree; the FAQ's is the fullest statement.
- **Cash pool.** [devpost] says "over $700,000 in cash prizes"; [home] says
  "$740k+ in cash prizes". [faq] (Prizes) explains that Devpost "only shows
  the prize money we have assigned to specific categories so far". Summing
  the cash amounts in the [devpost] Prizes list gives $740,000 (my
  arithmetic, not a quoted figure), so the two figures agree once all
  categories are listed. Quote "over $700,000" as the Devpost figure.
- **"All Other Categories" amounts.** [devpost] Prize structure says every
  other category pays "1st Place: $20,000", "2nd Place: $10,000", "3rd
  Place: $5,000". The detailed [devpost] Prizes list, which that section
  calls "the official details of prizes", pays $15,000 for 1st place in Ship
  Kotlin Everywhere, Most Viral App, Idea to Income, Growth Loop, and Funnel
  Vision, and lists no cash for Best App for Galaxy (one winner, perks only).
  The detailed Prizes list is authoritative.
- **Headline prize figure.** "over $700,000 in cash" and "over $1 million
  worth of prizes in total" ([devpost]; [blog-2026]); "$740k+ in cash
  prizes" ([home]); "$1M in prizes" ([vibe-code]).
- **Monetization requirement.** [devpost] The challenge: "at least one in-app
  purchase or serve ads through RevenueCat Ads"; [devpost] Requirements: "at
  least one in-app or web purchase" (no ads option); [faq] Submissions: "for
  in-app purchases, subscriptions, or ads". Treat IAP, subscriptions, web
  purchases, and RevenueCat Ads as accepted; the official rules decide.
- **One purchase: capability or transaction?** [devpost] asks that the SDK
  "power at least one in-app purchase"; [vibe-code] says "Get one sale, even
  from a friend or family member, or run a single ad. Either one meets the
  requirement." It is unclear whether a real transaction is required. Ship
  Kit's "First real purchase" milestone ([ship-kit]) and the Grand Prize's
  traction focus make one real purchase a safe target.
- **Platforms.** [devpost] Requirements: "iOS, iPadOS, macOS, or Android";
  [faq] Submissions: "iOS, macOS, or Android apps" (no iPadOS). Devpost is
  authoritative.
- **Store list.** [devpost] and [faq] name the App Store, Google Play, and

  Compatible, since Devpost allows macOS apps.

  Requirements accepts "in-app or web purchase", Funnel Vision requires a
  "live web funnel", and [faq] lets a web-only project enter with its first
  native store release. Reading: the entry must be a native store app; web
  payments are allowed as monetization.
- **Required submission fields.** [devpost] (What to submit) lists six
  items. [blog-submit] lists nine and says missing any makes the app "not
  eligible for judging", adding the project name and tagline, the
  "RevenueCat project ID", and category-specific details; [blog-judge]
  intake also checks "a valid bundle ID or package name". Provide all of
  them; the Devpost form itself is the final word.
- **Funnel Vision fields.** [cat-stripe]: "No additional sponsor-specific
  submission fields have been announced." [blog-submit] (Aug 27, 2026):
  "Include the funnel URL and Stripe Project ID." The category page is
  undated, so which came first is unknown; include both.
- **Video length (2025 vs 2026).** [win-4] (2025) says judges watch "max
  three minutes"; the 2026 rule is "no longer than 2 minutes of essential
  footage — **judges are not required to watch beyond two minutes.**"
  ([devpost]; also [blog-submit], [blog-judge]). Use two minutes.
- **Next Gen store link.** [devpost] says "Every submission must include" a

  entrant, may compete only for the Next Gen Award." Follow the FAQ.
- **Next Gen student proof.** [devpost] Judging criteria: "a .edu (or
  equivalent) email address"; [faq]: "a verifiable academic email address
  from a high school, college, university, bootcamp, or another academic
  program". Compatible; the FAQ is more specific.
- **How many categories to enter.** [vibe-code]: "Choose every category that
  fits your app." [blog-submit]: "don't try to target each category";
  [blog-judge]: "don't try to jam your app into every prize category."
