# Shipaton 2026 gallery landscape

A map of the public RevenueCat Shipaton 2026 project gallery, read in full on
September 22, 2026, when it listed 1,115 projects. It sorts every project into
an idea cluster, counts the entries that appear to aim at each prize category
in the [brief], and flags crowded and open idea spaces. Counts are what the
gallery and its search showed; judgment starts with "Synthesis:".

## Method and coverage

Every gallery page was read on September 22, 2026 with WebFetch, because
Devpost answers `curl` with HTTP 403.

- Pages: `?page=1` through `?page=48`. Pages 1 to 46 held 24 projects each,
  page 47 held 11, and page 48 said "There are no submissions which match your
  criteria." The count line ran without a gap from "1 – 24 of 1115" to
  "1105 – 1115 of 1115", and the 1,115 rows have 1,115 distinct project URLs.
  Every page is linked below.
- Listing: each tile shows a title, a tagline, likes, and comments. No tile
  showed built-with tags, prize labels, or a winner banner, so there were none
  to record.
- Controls: a "Filter submissions" panel with one question, "What type of app
  did you build? (select all that apply)", and the options "iOS (iPhone and/or
  iPad)", "Android", and "Mac"; a sort with "Name" and "Recent"; and a keyword
  search at `/submissions/search?terms=`. The filter's field names aren't in
  the fetched page, and guessed parameters returned either every project or
  none, so this note has no platform counts. ([gallery], [s-widget])
- Search: Devpost's help says the gallery search "indexes the project's title,
  description, sponsor prizes, answers to custom questions fields, and screen
  names". A hit means the term appears somewhere in that text, including
  answers that aren't public; it doesn't mean the project entered a category.
  Some stories do name their targets: the story of Is My Food Recalled? has
  sections headed "RevenueCat Peace Prize", "Catvertising Award", "HAMM
  Award", and three more. Phrases were searched in double quotes, and each
  count links to its search.
  ([dp-gallery-help], [p-foodrecall])
- Clusters: one reader gave each project one primary cluster from its title
  and tagline, using search hits to break ties. Influencer Award fits were
  judged against the wording of each category page.
- Renamed projects: for 48 projects the URL slug shares no word with the
  title. Two of them, Wakely: Alarm Clock to Wake Up and SnapSeries: Camera
  Overlay, were opened, and each page showed the listed title under that
  slug. ([p-wakely], [p-snapseries])

## Gallery totals

- Projects: 1,115 on September 22, 2026. The first read of page 1 said
  "1 – 24 of 1114", and every page of the full sweep said "of 1115"; the
  projects on page 1 didn't change between the two reads. ([gallery])
- Participants: the same page showed 26,937, so about 4% of registrants had a
  public project eight days before the September 30 deadline. ([gallery])
- Growth: the 2025 gallery ended at 813 projects, so the 2026 gallery already
  holds 302 more. ([past-winners])
- Games: 148 projects (13.3%) sit in the seven game clusters, or 158 with game
  backlogs and trackers.
- Placeholders: 20 entries (1.8%) are placeholders or too vague to place, such
  as Sample ("for test") and qwq ("placeholder").
- Category names: 164 projects (14.7%) name at least one prize category in
  their searchable text, across 16 category-name searches, and 626 (56.1%)
  mention "paywall". ([s-paywall])
- Likes: 353 projects have at least one like, 517 likes in all; the most
  liked, Aýkitap, has 18. ([gallery])

Synthesis: the field is broad and flat. No cluster holds more than 4.1% of
the gallery, so crowding shows up in narrow sub-spaces and in two influencer
briefs rather than in whole categories.

## Influencer Award briefs

"Direct" means the tagline offers what the brief asks for; "adjacent" means
the same space without the brief's core requirement. Quotes are taglines.
Influencer entries are private, and a project may enter only one Influencer
Award, so these are fits, not entries. ([brief])

### Productivity brief (Christopher Lawley)

The brief: "Build an app that brings text snippets, images, files, and
documents together in one fast, focused place." ([cat-lawley])

Direct fits, 6:

- Amber: Snippets, Images, Docs: "Snippets, images and documents — saved in a
  tap, found in a keystroke".
- SnipStack: "One fast, focused place to save and retrieve reusable snippets —
  text, links, images, and files."
- SaveCross: Clipboard Library: "Save text, links, images, and files once".
- PasteKit: "Save text and images once, then paste them anywhere from your
  keyboard."
- Stash: Quickly Add to Clipboard: "Quickly access your text snippets, links,
  photos, and docs."; the only hit for "lawley" ([s-lawley]).
- Shelvr: "Save links, screenshots, photos, and notes in one place."

Adjacent, 14: snippet or clipboard tools for one kind of content (SnapStash,
Snugit, SnapClip — Smart Snippet & Clipboard Vault, PromptVault Pro,
ClipStack, SnippetVault, and ClipPool – clipboard history manager for macOS),
capture inboxes and savers (Assorta, taggy, Echo, Bookmark Manager: Nemor, and
The Lede), and document vaults (Doksy: Private Family ID Keeper and Life
Vault : Digital Locker).

Search: "snippets" [15][s-snippets], "snippet" [19][s-snippet], and
"clipboard" [16][s-clipboard].

Synthesis: crowded. Six entries pitch the brief almost word for word, and
SnipStack reuses its "one fast, focused place". None of the 20 taglines names
the brief's own examples: "canned email replies, documents someone regularly
shows to clients, and information used to fill out forms".

### Nutrition & Healthy Eating brief (Abbey's Kitchen)

The brief: help people answer "What can we add to make this meal more
satisfying?", and the app "must not rely on calorie counting or macro
tracking". ([cat-abbey])

Direct fits, 4:

- Tonight: "not a calorie count anywhere"; the only hit for "hunger crushing"
  ([s-hunger]).
- Add One Thing: "It tells you one thing to add that will make the meal
  actually fill you up."
- Krasis: "the one thing from your own kitchen to add if it won't. No
  calories."
- Same Table: Add One, Say One: "One food idea for the meal already in front
  of you, at a restaurant, on the road, with kids or with leftovers."

Names the brief but pitches a planner, 1: Bite Club - Family Meal Planner,
which search ties to "abbey" and to "influencer award" ([s-abbey],
[s-influencer-award]).

Adjacent, 4: food logs that skip counting, namely Crumbo ("No calories. No
guilt."), Bite° Food Tracker ("Don't count calorites - collect bites."),
PlateDiary - Food Journaling, and BioTile: Build your Microbiome.

Against the brief: 15 calorie or macro counters, such as CalBud: AI Calorie
Counter and BulkUp AI - Calorie Tracker. Search: "calorie" [44][s-calorie],
"macros" [23][s-macros], and "meal plan" [15][s-meal-plan].

Synthesis: thin but converging. Three of the four direct fits share one
mechanic, "add one thing", and among them only Same Table pitches the brief's
messy situations of restaurants, trips, kids, and leftovers.

### Career Coaching brief (Leadership Heather)

The brief: somewhere new managers "can practise feedback, boundaries, saying
no, and other high-stakes conversations", a "flight simulator for hard
conversations". ([cat-heather])

Direct fits, 14, all conversation-rehearsal apps:

- Aimed at managers, 6: Unawkward: Manager Coaching; ManagerGym; Rehearse;
  SayBetter AI ("lets new managers rehearse feedback, boundaries and saying no
  out loud"); KindTake: Leadership Simulator ("Pilots use simulators before
  flying; managers should too."); and Rehearso: Leadership Coach.
- Aimed at workplace conversations, 2: ReplayLead and BraveLine.
- Aimed at any hard conversation, 6: PeaceSpeaker, NextSay, Hard
  Conversation, Firmly, DryRun: Say the Hard Thing, and Tablework.

Adjacent, 5: Kirokune (a log of difficult workplace experiences), re:speech©️|
Turn ideas into impact., Orati - Visual Speech Practice Timer, Green Room:
Talk Day Prep, and ReplyRight.

Search: "heather" [6][s-heather], "flight simulator" [2][s-flight-sim]
(Firmly and KindTake), "hard conversations" [8][s-hard-conv], "difficult
conversations" [13][s-difficult-conv], and "new managers"
[6][s-new-managers]. Rehearso and PeaceSpeaker are also hits for "influencer
award" ([s-influencer-award]).

Synthesis: the most crowded brief. Fourteen rehearsal apps chase one award
and six already pitch managers, so the idea itself is taken; a new entry
would have to win on realism of the scenarios or quality of the feedback.

## Limitations

- Snapshot: the gallery was read once, on September 22, 2026. It lists only
  reviewed submissions, hides edited ones until they're reviewed again, and
  will grow before September 30, so every count is a floor.
- Transcription: WebFetch reads pages through a model. Rows were checked for
  count, field shape, and unique URLs, not character by character.
- Clusters: one reader chose one primary cluster per project from its title
  and tagline. Many projects span clusters; LangCity, a pixel-art RPG for
  learning Japanese, counts under language learning, not games.
- Search: hits count mentions in text that includes answers the public can't
  see, so a hit isn't an entry and a miss isn't proof of absence. "noise",
  "layers", "galaxy", "next gen", "student", and "influencer" all have other
  meanings.
- Category names: 12 projects name five or more categories, which inflates
  the "names it" counts.
- Coverage: the platform filter couldn't be driven, the listing shows no
  built-with tags, and only four project pages were opened, so platform and
  technology counts come from search alone.
